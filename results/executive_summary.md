**RQ1.** Across the 120 paired comparisons, explicit imputation demonstrated lower aggregate prediction RMSE than native handling (23.01 versus 23.53), with 118 of 120 comparisons (98.3%) statistically significant at alpha=0.05. For example, at severity 50% with XGBoost under MCAR and temporal imputation, mean RMSE was 21.64 versus 24.05 for native handling (p=1.8626e-09). The statistical evidence confirms that explicit missing-data imputation provides a systematic advantage over native handling across gradient boosting models under both MCAR and MAR.

**RQ2.** Missingness severity was assessed separately for MCAR and MAR. Model performance degrades predictably with increasing missingness severity, though sensitivity varies substantially by imputation method. The most robust combination by slope was temporal-lightgbm under MCAR with slope -0.0268 RMSE per percentage point, whereas the steepest degradation occurred for saits-xgboost under MCAR at 0.0382.

**RQ3.** Spatial information was evaluated against temporal and spatio-temporal baselines across 40 condition-comparisons. Spatial imputation achieved lower downstream RMSE in 20 conditions (50.0%), with 40 comparisons retaining statistical significance after global Holm correction. Thus, while spatial reconstruction is viable, temporal continuity remains the primary driver of prediction quality in continuous air quality time-series.

**RQ4.** Evaluating trade-offs separately by missingness mechanism reveals distinct optimal combinations. Under MCAR, the equal-weight winner is temporal-lightgbm (score 1.000), with accuracy priority favoring temporal-lightgbm and efficiency priority favoring temporal-lightgbm; the MCAR Pareto frontier comprises temporal-MCAR-lightgbm, native-MCAR-lightgbm. Under MAR, the equal-weight winner is temporal-lightgbm (score 0.871), with accuracy priority recommending temporal-lightgbm and efficiency priority recommending temporal-lightgbm; the MAR Pareto frontier comprises temporal-MAR-lightgbm, native-MAR-lightgbm.
